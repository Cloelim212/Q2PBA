The Mathematics Behind Baking

Math is useful in baking because the amount of ingredients, preparation time, and size of baked goods can change depending on what we need. We can use variation and radicals to solve these kinds of problems. These examples show how it can be applied in real-life baking.

Problem 1 — Direct Variation

A recipe uses 200 grams of flour for 8 cupcakes. How much flour is needed for 20 cupcakes?
We use the formula: y = kx
Since k wasn't given, let's find k using the example given.
200 grams of flour makes 8 cupcakes, so let's divide 200 by 8.
200 ÷ 8 = 25
So that means k = 25.
Now let's go back to our main problem and use our newly found k.
y = (25)(20)
y = 500

So that means we need 500 grams of flour to make 20 cupcakes.

Problem 2 — Inverse Variation

Two bakers can finish decorating a batch of cupcakes in 6 hours. If 4 bakers work at the same rate, how long will it take?
We use the formula: y = k/x
Since k wasn't given, let's find k using the information given.
We have 2 bakers and it takes them 6 hours, so we multiply 2 by 6.
k = (2)(6)
k = 12
So that means k = 12.
Now let's go back to our main problem and use 4 bakers.
y = 12/4
y = 3
So that means 4 bakers will take 3 hours to finish decorating the cupcakes.

Problem 3 — Radicals

A square cake has an area of 288 cm². What is the length of one side of the cake?
We use the formula: A = s²
Since the area is 288 cm², we replace A with 288.
288 = s²
To find s, we take the square root of 288.
s = √288
Now let's simplify √288.
√288 = √(144 × 2)
√288 = 12√2
12√2 is approximately 16.97.
So that means one side of the square cake is 12√2 cm, or about 17 cm.
